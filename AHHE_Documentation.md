# Amdavad Heritage Homestay Exchange (AHHE)
## Complete Product & Engineering Documentation

---

# 1. Project Overview

Amdavad Heritage Homestay Exchange (AHHE) is a **community-first, micro-hospitality marketplace** focused exclusively on the UNESCO World Heritage Old City of Ahmedabad — the *Pols* (traditional residential clusters). It connects heritage homeowners who have spare rooms, courtyards, or havelis with travelers who want an authentic, culturally immersive stay, especially during high-demand festival windows such as Uttarayan (kite festival), Navratri, and Diwali.

Unlike Airbnb or OYO, AHHE is **hyper-local, hyper-seasonal, and heritage-preservation-oriented**. It is not trying to be a global travel platform — it is trying to be the definitive booking layer for one specific, culturally rich, geographically bounded neighborhood cluster. This narrow focus is a strength, not a limitation: it allows for deep trust-building, local partnerships, city-government alignment (heritage tourism boards), and a defensible niche that large players ignore because it's too small for them to bother localizing for.

At a beginner level: think of AHHE as "Airbnb, but only for the old walled city of Ahmedabad, run with the community's own trust systems, lower fees, and festival-specific demand spikes built into the product."

At an advanced level: AHHE is a **two-sided marketplace with seasonal demand elasticity**, requiring dynamic pricing, trust/verification infrastructure for informal housing stock, hyperlocal logistics (Pols have narrow lanes, no vehicle access, GPS ambiguity), and a monetization model that must work even though transaction volume is concentrated in 3-4 short annual windows.

# 2. Problem Statement

### The Core Problem
Old Ahmedabad's Pols contain thousands of heritage havelis, many under-monetized or falling into disrepair because owners lack modern income streams to maintain them. Meanwhile:

- **Demand side pain:** Tourists and heritage enthusiasts visiting during Navratri, Uttarayan, and Diwali cannot find authentic Pol accommodation — they end up staying in generic hotels far from the old city, missing the actual cultural experience they came for.
- **Supply side pain:** Homeowners with spare rooms have no easy, trustworthy, low-fee way to list their homes. Existing OTAs (Online Travel Agencies) either ignore this informal housing stock (no star ratings, no formal registration) or charge fees (15-25%) that make it not worth the hassle for a homeowner renting a room for 3-4 nights a year.
- **Heritage preservation pain:** Without new income, many families sell ancestral homes to developers, accelerating the loss of Pol architecture. A functioning micro-hospitality economy gives families a financial reason to preserve rather than sell.
- **Trust & informality pain:** Informal home-sharing in a dense old-city context has real safety, verification, and dispute-resolution problems that generic marketplaces don't solve well for first-time hosts.

### Why Existing Platforms Fail Here
- **Airbnb/Booking.com:** Built for global scale, not hyperlocal narrow-lane navigation, not built to specifically court heritage-conscious travelers with contextual storytelling (which Pol, which era, which craft tradition).
- **OYO/Hotel aggregators:** Designed for standardized inventory (AC, attached bath, branded quality) — heritage havelis are the opposite: idiosyncratic, non-standardized, culturally rich but operationally "imperfect" by hotel standards.
- **Word of mouth / offline booking:** Currently the dominant channel — unscalable, no price discovery, no safety verification, no digital payment trail.

# 3. Target Audience

### Supply Side (Hosts)
- Heritage homeowners in Pols (joint families, elderly owner-occupiers, absentee owners with caretakers)
- Trust/NGO-managed heritage properties looking for supplementary income
- Second-generation owners who've partially renovated homes and want structured income

### Demand Side (Guests)
- **Cultural tourists** (domestic + international) attending Navratri/Uttarayan/Diwali
- **Heritage & architecture enthusiasts** (photographers, researchers, architecture students)
- **Diaspora Gujaratis** returning for festivals who want an immersive experience rather than staying with distant relatives or in hotels
- **Corporate/CSR heritage tourism groups** and boutique travel agencies curating Ahmedabad heritage circuits
- **Academic groups** (UNESCO heritage studies, urban planning students)

### Tertiary Stakeholders
- Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation (AMC) Heritage Cell
- Gujarat Tourism Board
- Local guides, food vendors, artisans who benefit from increased footfall
- Heritage conservation NGOs (e.g., groups modeled on CEPT's heritage walk initiatives)

# 4. Real-World Use Cases

1. **Uttarayan (Kite Festival) Rooftop Stay:** A Pol homeowner lists a room with rooftop (agasi) access specifically for Uttarayan — guests get a bed AND the culturally iconic experience of kite-flying from a heritage rooftop, something no hotel can offer.
2. **Navratri Garba Homestay Package:** A host bundles a room with walking access to a community Garba ground, positioning the listing as "5 min walk to traditional Garba" — a location-specific value hotels 8km away cannot replicate.
3. **Diwali Family Immersion:** A family hosts guests for Diwali rituals (rangoli-making, traditional sweets, puja) — an "experience-inclusive" booking, not just a room.
4. **Off-season Heritage Walk Base:** Outside festival season, architecture students/photographers book multi-night stays as a base for heritage walks, keeping some off-season revenue flowing.
5. **Emergency Overflow During Mega Events:** When Ahmedabad hosts large events (International Kite Festival, heritage festivals) and hotels are fully booked, AHHE becomes the release-valve inventory the city itself may informally point tourists toward.
6. **Diaspora Reunion Hosting:** NRIs returning for family events use AHHE to house extended family/guests in the same Pol as relatives, rather than a distant hotel.

# 5. Vision & Long-Term Goal

**Short-term vision (Year 1-2):** Become the trusted digital booking layer for Pol homestays during Ahmedabad's three major festivals, with a critical mass of ~150-300 verified listings.

**Mid-term vision (Year 3-5):** Expand to year-round heritage tourism (not just festivals), add curated "experiences" (heritage walks, cooking classes, craft workshops) alongside stays, and become a revenue partner for heritage conservation — a percentage of platform revenue could fund a **Pol Heritage Preservation Fund**.

**Long-term vision (Year 5+):** Replicate the model in other UNESCO/heritage old-city clusters in India (Jaipur's Pols equivalent — havelis, Varanasi's old ghats, Kutch's heritage villages) under a shared "Heritage Homestay Exchange" platform franchise/playbook, with AHHE as the flagship proof-of-concept.

**North Star Metric:** Number of heritage homes kept in family ownership and actively maintained *because of* platform-generated income (a preservation-outcome metric, not just GMV).

# 6. Unique Selling Point (USP)

1. **Hyperlocal exclusivity** — the only platform built specifically for Pol architecture and festival-driven demand, not a generic listing shoved into a "heritage" filter on a global OTA.
2. **Low-fee, community-first economics** — dramatically lower commission than Airbnb/OYO (target 5-8% vs 15-25%), because the platform doesn't need to subsidize global marketing — it wins on local trust and SEO for "Ahmedabad heritage homestay."
3. **Festival-native product design** — calendar, pricing, and search are built around Uttarayan/Navratri/Diwali as first-class concepts, not generic date-pickers.
4. **Cultural verification layer** — hosts can be verified not just for identity/safety but for authentic heritage character (photos of architecture, family history blurb, craft traditions) — a "heritage authenticity badge."
5. **Preservation-linked mission** — a portion of revenue/optional guest micro-donation goes to a heritage preservation fund, giving the platform a values-based brand story that differentiates it from purely commercial OTAs.

# 7. Core Features

- **Listing Management:** Hosts create listings with photos, description, heritage story, room type, amenities, pricing, and festival-specific availability.
- **Search & Discovery:** Filter by Pol/area, festival window, price, amenities (AC, attached bath, rooftop access), group size.
- **Booking Engine:** Date-based (and festival-package-based) booking with real-time availability calendar.
- **Secure Payments:** Escrow-style payment holding until check-in confirmation, with UPI/card support (India-first payment rails).
- **Reviews & Ratings:** Two-way reviews (guest rates host, host rates guest) for trust building.
- **Messaging System:** In-platform chat between guest and host pre/post booking (no phone number exposure until booking confirmed, for safety).
- **Verification System:** ID verification for both guests and hosts; heritage-authenticity verification for listings (photo/documentation review).
- **Festival Calendar Integration:** Platform-wide calendar highlighting Uttarayan/Navratri/Diwali dates with surge-aware pricing suggestions.
- **Multi-language Support:** Gujarati, Hindi, English (critical for local host adoption and international guest reach).
- **Host Dashboard:** Booking requests, earnings, calendar management, payout tracking.
- **Guest Dashboard:** Booking history, upcoming trips, saved/wishlisted listings.

# 8. Advanced Features

- **Dynamic/Seasonal Pricing Engine:** Suggests price ranges based on historical festival demand, nearby listing prices, and days-to-event countdown.
- **Experience Add-ons Marketplace:** Hosts or local guides can offer add-on experiences (heritage walk, cooking class, kite-making workshop) bundled with stays.
- **Heritage Storytelling CMS:** Rich media pages per Pol (history, architecture notes, community info) that listings link into — improves SEO and cultural depth.
- **Trust Score Algorithm:** Composite score from verification level, review history, response rate, cancellation rate.
- **Dispute Resolution Workflow:** Structured mediation flow (evidence submission, admin review, partial refund logic) for booking conflicts.
- **AI-powered Smart Search:** Natural language search ("rooftop room near Navratri Garba ground under ₹2000") mapped to structured filters.
- **Multi-listing Household Management:** Some Pol homes have multiple rentable rooms managed by one family — supports a "property with multiple units" hierarchy.
- **Waitlist & Notify-Me:** For sold-out festival dates, guests join a waitlist and get notified on cancellations.
- **Local Partner Integrations:** Auto-suggest local food vendors, auto-rickshaw pickup points (since cars can't enter narrow Pol lanes), given GPS ambiguity in old-city lanes.
- **Heritage Preservation Fund Tracker:** Public dashboard showing cumulative contributions and which restoration projects were funded.
- **Admin Analytics Suite:** Demand heatmaps by Pol, occupancy forecasting for festival windows, host onboarding funnel analytics.

# 9. MVP Features (Minimum Viable Product)

To launch fast and validate the core hypothesis (will hosts list, will guests book) without over-engineering:

1. Host signup + listing creation (manual admin verification, not automated)
2. Guest signup + browse/search by area & date
3. Booking request → host accept/reject (no instant-book initially, to build trust manually)
4. Basic payment collection (UPI/Razorpay integration, simple hold-and-release, not full escrow automation)
5. Basic in-app messaging
6. Simple review system (star rating + text, post-stay only)
7. Admin panel to manually verify hosts/listings (heritage-authenticity done by a human team initially, not AI, since volume is small at launch)
8. Festival calendar as a static filter (Uttarayan/Navratri/Diwali date ranges hardcoded initially)
9. Mobile-responsive web app (no native app needed for MVP — web-first, especially since older heritage homeowners may be more comfortable with a simple mobile browser flow than app installs)

**MVP Explicitly Excludes:** dynamic pricing AI, experience marketplace, multi-language at launch (start with English + Gujarati only), native mobile apps, automated ID verification (KYC APIs) — these are Phase 2+.

# 10. User Roles & Permissions

| Role | Description | Key Permissions |
|---|---|---|
| **Guest** | Traveler booking stays | Search, book, message hosts, review after stay, manage own profile/bookings |
| **Host** | Heritage homeowner listing rooms | Create/edit listings, accept/reject bookings, message guests, view earnings, set pricing/availability |

| **Super Admin** | Platform owner/founder team | Full system config, financial reporting, payout approval, role management |

Permission model should be implemented as **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)** with a `roles` and `permissions` join table (explained further in Database Schema section), not hardcoded role checks in application code — this makes future roles (e.g., "Local Partner") addable without code rewrites.

# 11. Functional Requirements

- FR1: Users can register/login via email, phone (OTP), or Google OAuth.
- FR2: Hosts can create, edit, deactivate listings with photos (min 1, max 15), pricing, and availability calendar.
- FR3: Guests can search listings filtered by Pol/area, date range, price range, amenities, and festival tag.
- FR4: System must prevent double-booking of the same room for overlapping date ranges (booking conflict validation at DB transaction level).
- FR5: Guests can send a booking request; hosts can accept/reject within a configurable time window (e.g., 24 hours), after which the request auto-expires.
- FR6: Payment is captured on booking confirmation and held until 24 hours post-check-in (configurable) before host payout release.
- FR7: Both parties can cancel per a defined cancellation policy (tiered refund based on days-before-check-in).
- FR8: Post-stay, both guest and host can leave a review within a 14-day window; reviews are mutually blind (not visible to either party until both submit or window expires) to reduce retaliatory bias.
- FR9: Admin can suspend/ban users or listings that violate policy.
- FR10: System sends automated notifications (email/SMS/push) for booking events (request, confirmation, reminder, cancellation).
- FR11: Hosts can view an earnings dashboard with payout history and pending payouts.
- FR12: System supports multi-currency display for international guests (display only; settlement in INR).

---

# 12. Non-Functional Requirements

- **Performance:** Search results should return in <500ms for 95th percentile queries; page load (LCP) under 2.5s on 4G mobile.
- **Scalability:** System must handle festival-season traffic spikes (e.g., 20-50x normal traffic in the 2 weeks before Uttarayan) without degradation — this is a defining NFR unique to AHHE's seasonal business model.
- **Availability:** 99.5%+ uptime target (not 99.99% — early-stage startup, cost-appropriate target), with priority uptime guarantees specifically during the 3 festival windows.
- **Security:** All PII encrypted at rest; payment data never touches AHHE's own servers directly (delegated to PCI-DSS compliant payment gateway like Razorpay/Stripe).
- **Data Privacy:** Compliance with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP) 2023 — consent-based data collection, right to erasure.
- **Localization:** UI must support Gujarati script rendering correctly (font/encoding considerations).
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.1 AA compliance for core booking flows (screen-reader friendly, keyboard navigable).
- **Maintainability:** Modular codebase with >70% test coverage on core booking/payment logic.
- **Usability:** Host onboarding flow must be completable by a non-technical, possibly older user in under 10 minutes (critical since many heritage homeowners are not digitally native).

### Architecture Explanation (Beginner to Advanced)

**Beginner explanation:** Think of the system like a restaurant. The client (web app) is the dining area where customers sit. The API gateway is the host stand that directs each request to the right department (kitchen, bar, billing). Each "service" (Auth, Booking, Notifications) is a specialized department, and the database is the pantry where all data/ingredients are stored.

**Advanced explanation:** This is a **modular monolith transitioning toward service-oriented architecture** — appropriate for AHHE's scale. A full microservices architecture (separate deployable services with independent databases) would be over-engineering at MVP stage — the operational overhead (service discovery, distributed tracing, inter-service auth) isn't justified by AHHE's expected traffic (thousands, not millions, of daily active users). Instead:

- Start as a **modular monolith**: one deployable backend application, internally organized into clear modules (Auth, Booking, Listings, Payments, Notifications) with well-defined internal interfaces.
- This allows a **future extraction path**: if the Search or Notification module later needs independent scaling (e.g., search load grows disproportionately due to SEO traffic), it can be extracted into its own service without a full rewrite, because module boundaries were already respected.
- The **festival traffic spike** is the primary architectural stress case — the system must be able to horizontally scale the API layer and read-replica the database specifically in the 2-3 weeks surrounding each festival, then scale back down (cost efficiency).

# 14. Frontend Architecture (React + Vite, decoupled from Django backend)

```
React SPA (Vite build tool, TypeScript)
│
├── Public Routes (React Router)
│    ├── Landing Page
│    ├── Pol/Area Story Pages (heritage content)
│    ├── Listing Detail Pages
│    └── Search Results Page
│
├── Authenticated App Shell (route-guarded via JWT presence + role)
│    ├── Guest Dashboard
│    ├── Host Dashboard
│    ├── Booking Flow (multi-step form)
│    ├── Messaging Center
│    └── Admin Panel (separate route group, role-gated)
│
├── Shared Component Library (Design System)
│    ├── Atoms: Button, Input, Badge, Avatar
│    ├── Molecules: ListingCard, ReviewCard, DatePicker
│    ├── Organisms: SearchFilterBar, BookingSummaryPanel
│    └── Templates: DashboardLayout, PublicPageLayout
│
└── State Management: React Query / TanStack Query (server state) + Zustand (light client state)
```

**Why this structure:** With **Django + Django REST Framework (DRF)** as a fully decoupled backend, the frontend becomes a pure **client-rendered React SPA built with Vite** (much faster dev/build experience than Create React App, and simpler than Next.js when you don't need the backend/frontend coupled in one Node runtime). This is a clean **API-first split**: Django owns all business logic, auth, and data; React only consumes JSON over REST.

**Important SEO trade-off to know:** A pure client-rendered SPA is *not* great for Google indexing of public pages (listing pages, Pol heritage story pages), and organic search ("Ahmedabad heritage homestay Uttarayan") is a major low-cost acquisition channel for AHHE. Two practical options once this starts to matter:
1. **Django-rendered public pages:** Let Django serve server-rendered HTML (via Django templates) *only* for the SEO-critical public pages (listing detail, Pol story, landing), while the React SPA handles everything behind login (dashboards, booking flow, messaging). This is a common hybrid pattern and keeps the stack you asked for.
2. **Pre-rendering/SSG for public pages:** Use a static-site generator pass (e.g., `vite-plugin-ssr` or a simple prerendering step) for the small set of public marketing/listing pages, while the authenticated app stays a pure SPA.

For MVP, plain React SPA is perfectly fine — just flag this as a Phase 2 SEO fix once organic traffic becomes a real growth lever, not before.

Separating "Server State" (data from API — React Query handles caching/refetching) from "Client State" (UI toggles, form state — Zustand) avoids the common anti-pattern of dumping everything into one global Redux store.

# 15. Backend Architecture (Django + Django REST Framework)

**Layered architecture within the modular monolith:**

```
View/Serializer Layer (DRF ViewSets + Serializers) → Handles HTTP requests, input validation
        │
        ▼
Service Layer (plain Python service functions/classes, NOT fat models, NOT fat views)
        →  Core rules: booking conflict checks, pricing logic, cancellation policy calculations
        │
        ▼
Model/ORM Layer (Django ORM, QuerySets, Managers)
        │
        ▼
Database (PostgreSQL)
```

**Django app structure (each Django "app" = a bounded module, not a microservice):**
- `accounts`: registration, login, JWT issuance (via `djangorestframework-simplejwt`), OTP verification, OAuth (via `django-allauth`)
- `users`: profile CRUD, role management (custom `User` model + `Role`/`Permission` relations), verification status
- `listings`: CRUD for listings, photo upload orchestration (to S3 via `django-storages`), availability calendar logic
- `bookings`: booking request/accept/reject state machine, conflict-prevention transaction logic (using `select_for_update()` row locking + DB-level constraints)
- `payments`: integration with Razorpay/Stripe SDKs, webhook handling for payment status, payout scheduling
- `reviews`: mutual-blind review logic, rating aggregation
- `messaging`: chat thread management, message persistence, real-time delivery (Django Channels + WebSocket)
- `notifications`: templated email/SMS/push dispatch, event-driven triggers (via Celery tasks)
- `admin_ops`: verification workflows, dispute resolution — built on top of **Django Admin**, which is a major practical advantage of Django here (a working, permission-aware admin backoffice essentially for free, instead of hand-building an admin panel from scratch)
- `search`: query building for Elasticsearch or PostgreSQL full-text search (`django.contrib.postgres.search`)
- `analytics_ml`: houses analytics event ingestion and ML model-serving endpoints (see Section 51)

**Why Django specifically fits AHHE well:**
- **Django Admin** gives the Ops/verification team (Section 10's Admin role) a fully functional, permission-gated backoffice UI (approve hosts, review listings, resolve disputes) with almost no custom frontend work — a genuinely large time-saver for a small team, and directly useful for AHHE's manual-verification-first trust model (Section 27/48/49).
- **Django ORM + migrations** are mature and battle-tested for exactly the relational, transaction-heavy data model AHHE needs (booking conflict prevention, payment state).
- **Django REST Framework** gives fast, consistent REST API scaffolding (serializers, permissions, viewsets, pagination) matching the API design in Section 17.
- **Celery + Redis** (standard Django async task pattern) cleanly handles the async/queue-based work described in Section 28 (notifications, image processing, ML scoring jobs) without needing a separate Node-based queue system.
- **Django Channels** extends Django to handle the WebSocket-based real-time messaging (Section 15 `messaging` app) that a purely synchronous Django view can't.
- Django's batteries-included philosophy (auth, admin, ORM, forms/validation) reduces the number of third-party architectural decisions a small team has to make correctly on day one — a real advantage when the team is 1-2 backend engineers.

**Booking State Machine (critical business logic):**
```
[Requested] → (host accepts) → [Confirmed] → (check-in date passes) → [Completed]
     │                                │
     │ (host rejects/timeout)         │ (either party cancels)
     ▼                                ▼
[Rejected/Expired]              [Cancelled] → refund calculated per policy
```

This state machine should be implemented explicitly (not as loose boolean flags) — every transition should be validated server-side so an invalid transition (e.g., cancelling an already-completed booking) is rejected at the service layer, not just hidden in the UI.

# 16. Database Architecture

**Primary datastore:** PostgreSQL — chosen over NoSQL because AHHE's data is fundamentally **relational** (users, listings, bookings, payments, reviews all have strong foreign-key relationships and require transactional integrity, especially for preventing double-bookings).

**Supporting stores:**
- **Redis:** session cache, rate-limiting counters, real-time availability cache (to reduce DB load during festival-season search spikes), pub/sub for WebSocket message delivery across server instances.
- **Amazon S3 (or Cloudflare R2):** listing photos, verification documents, heritage-story media — never store binary files in the primary DB.
- **Elasticsearch (Phase 2, optional at MVP):** if PostgreSQL full-text search + filtering becomes insufficient for the "smart search" advanced feature, introduce Elasticsearch as a read-optimized search index, synced from PostgreSQL via change-data-capture or scheduled sync jobs.

**Read replica strategy:** As festival-season read traffic (search, browsing) spikes far more than write traffic (bookings), a **read replica** of PostgreSQL should be provisioned ahead of each festival season, with search/listing-browse queries routed to the replica and booking/payment writes routed to the primary.

# 17. API Architecture

**Style:** RESTful JSON API for MVP (simpler to build, cache, and document than GraphQL at this stage; GraphQL can be reconsidered in Phase 2 if frontend data-fetching complexity grows, e.g., many nested dashboard views).

**Versioning:** URL-based versioning (`/api/v1/...`) from day one, to avoid breaking changes for any future mobile app or third-party integration.

**Sample core endpoints:**
```
POST   /api/v1/auth/register
POST   /api/v1/auth/login
POST   /api/v1/auth/otp/verify

GET    /api/v1/listings                 (search/filter)
POST   /api/v1/listings                 (host creates)
GET    /api/v1/listings/:id
PATCH  /api/v1/listings/:id
DELETE /api/v1/listings/:id

POST   /api/v1/bookings                 (guest requests booking)
PATCH  /api/v1/bookings/:id/accept      (host accepts)
PATCH  /api/v1/bookings/:id/reject
PATCH  /api/v1/bookings/:id/cancel
GET    /api/v1/bookings/:id

POST   /api/v1/payments/create-order
POST   /api/v1/payments/webhook          (payment gateway callback)

POST   /api/v1/reviews
GET    /api/v1/listings/:id/reviews

GET    /api/v1/messages/:threadId
POST   /api/v1/messages

GET    /api/v1/admin/verifications/pending
PATCH  /api/v1/admin/verifications/:id/approve
```

**Standards:** Consistent error envelope (`{ error: { code, message, details } }`), pagination via cursor-based tokens for listing/search endpoints (more scalable than offset pagination at high volume), idempotency keys required on payment-creating endpoints to prevent double-charging on network retries.

# 18. Authentication & Authorization Flow

```
1. User submits phone number → OTP sent via Twilio/MSG91
2. User submits OTP → backend verifies → issues JWT access token (short-lived, 15 min)
                                        + refresh token (long-lived, 30 days, stored HttpOnly cookie)
3. Client stores access token in memory (NOT localStorage — XSS risk)
4. Each API request includes: Authorization: Bearer <access_token>
5. Middleware validates JWT signature + expiry → attaches decoded user + role to request context
6. Role-based guard checks: does this role have permission for this route/action?
7. On access token expiry: client silently calls /auth/refresh using the HttpOnly refresh cookie
8. On logout: refresh token is revoked server-side (stored in a revocation list/Redis)
```

**Why phone/OTP-first (not just email):** Many Pol homeowners are more comfortable and reachable via phone/WhatsApp-linked numbers than email — OTP-first auth matches the actual target user's digital habits, a UX decision as much as a technical one.

**Authorization model:** RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) at the route/middleware level, combined with **resource-ownership checks** at the service level (e.g., a host can only edit *their own* listings — this can't be captured by role alone, it needs an explicit `listing.hostId === request.user.id` check in the service layer).

# 20. Why Each Technology Should Be Used

- **React (Vite):** Fast dev/build tooling, huge ecosystem/component libraries, and a natural fit for a decoupled SPA talking to a Django REST API — simpler mental model than a full-stack Node framework when the backend is Python.
- **TypeScript (frontend):** Strong typing catches integration bugs against the DRF API contract early, especially valuable for a small team without a large QA function; pair with auto-generated API types (e.g., via `drf-spectacular`'s OpenAPI schema + `openapi-typescript`) so the frontend types stay in sync with Django's actual API shape.
- **Django + DRF:** Batteries-included framework (auth, ORM, admin, migrations, forms/validation) that lets a small backend team move fast without re-deciding foundational architecture choices; DRF's serializers/viewsets/permissions map cleanly onto the modular-monolith app structure described in Section 15.
- **Django Admin:** Effectively a free, permission-aware backoffice UI for the Ops/verification team — directly accelerates AHHE's manual-verification-first trust workflow without custom admin-panel development.
- **PostgreSQL:** ACID transactions are non-negotiable for booking-conflict prevention (you cannot double-book a room) — a NoSQL document store would require reinventing transactional guarantees; Django ORM's `select_for_update()` and PostgreSQL's `EXCLUDE` constraints work together well here.
- **Celery + Redis:** Standard, mature Django pattern for async/background work (notifications, image processing, ML scoring, payout scheduling) — avoids blocking the request/response cycle during festival-season load.
- **Django Channels:** Adds WebSocket support to Django for the real-time messaging feature without needing a separate Node.js service just for chat.
- **Razorpay:** Best India-market payment gateway support (UPI, native wallets), lower fees than Stripe for INR transactions, faster payout settlement to Indian bank accounts — directly relevant since AHHE's hosts need fast, trusted payouts to build confidence in a new platform.
- **Railway/Render (early stage):** Both React static builds and Django + Celery + Postgres + Redis deploy cleanly here with minimal DevOps overhead; migrate to AWS/Terraform once traffic and team size justify the operational complexity.
- **Docker:** Ensures dev/staging/production environment parity — critical when the team is small and can't afford "works on my machine" debugging cycles, and especially useful for keeping Python dependency versions consistent.
- **Sentry:** Fast error visibility (with Django + React SDKs) is disproportionately valuable during festival-season traffic spikes, when a bug can directly cost bookings during the only high-revenue weeks of the year.

# 21. Database Schema Design

```sql
-- USERS
users (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  full_name VARCHAR(150) NOT NULL,
  phone VARCHAR(15) UNIQUE NOT NULL,
  email VARCHAR(150) UNIQUE,
  password_hash VARCHAR(255),          -- nullable if OTP-only
  profile_photo_url TEXT,
  preferred_language VARCHAR(10) DEFAULT 'en',
  is_phone_verified BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
  is_id_verified BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
  created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now(),
  updated_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now()
)

-- ROLES & PERMISSIONS (RBAC)
roles ( id UUID PRIMARY KEY, name VARCHAR(50) UNIQUE )  -- guest, host, admin, super_admin
user_roles ( user_id UUID REFERENCES users(id), role_id UUID REFERENCES roles(id), PRIMARY KEY(user_id, role_id) )

-- LISTINGS
listings (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  host_id UUID REFERENCES users(id),
  title VARCHAR(200) NOT NULL,
  description TEXT,
  heritage_story TEXT,
  pol_name VARCHAR(150) NOT NULL,      -- e.g., "Mangaldas Pol"
  address_line TEXT,
  latitude DECIMAL(9,6),
  longitude DECIMAL(9,6),
  price_per_night DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
  max_guests INT NOT NULL,
  room_type VARCHAR(50),               -- private_room, entire_haveli, shared_room
  amenities JSONB,                     -- flexible: {ac: true, attached_bath: true, rooftop: true}
  heritage_verified BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
  status VARCHAR(20) DEFAULT 'active', -- active, inactive, under_review
  created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now(),
  updated_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now()
)

listing_photos (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  listing_id UUID REFERENCES listings(id),
  photo_url TEXT NOT NULL,
  display_order INT DEFAULT 0
)

listing_availability (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  listing_id UUID REFERENCES listings(id),
  date DATE NOT NULL,
  is_available BOOLEAN DEFAULT TRUE,
  custom_price DECIMAL(10,2),          -- overrides default price (e.g., festival surge)
  UNIQUE(listing_id, date)
)

-- BOOKINGS
bookings (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  listing_id UUID REFERENCES listings(id),
  guest_id UUID REFERENCES users(id),
  check_in DATE NOT NULL,
  check_out DATE NOT NULL,
  guest_count INT NOT NULL,
  total_price DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
  status VARCHAR(20) DEFAULT 'requested', -- requested, confirmed, rejected, cancelled, completed
  festival_tag VARCHAR(50),               -- uttarayan, navratri, diwali, none
  created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now(),
  CONSTRAINT no_overlap EXCLUDE USING gist (
     listing_id WITH =,
     daterange(check_in, check_out) WITH &&
  ) WHERE (status IN ('requested','confirmed'))
)

-- PAYMENTS
payments (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  booking_id UUID REFERENCES bookings(id),
  amount DECIMAL(10,2) NOT NULL,
  gateway_order_id VARCHAR(150),
  gateway_payment_id VARCHAR(150),
  status VARCHAR(20) DEFAULT 'pending',  -- pending, captured, refunded, failed
  payout_status VARCHAR(20) DEFAULT 'held', -- held, released, cancelled
  created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now()
)

-- REVIEWS
reviews (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  booking_id UUID REFERENCES bookings(id),
  reviewer_id UUID REFERENCES users(id),
  reviewee_id UUID REFERENCES users(id),
  rating INT CHECK (rating BETWEEN 1 AND 5),
  comment TEXT,
  is_visible BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,   -- becomes true only when both sides submitted / window expired
  created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now()
)

-- MESSAGING
message_threads ( id UUID PRIMARY KEY, booking_id UUID REFERENCES bookings(id), created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now() )
messages (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  thread_id UUID REFERENCES message_threads(id),
  sender_id UUID REFERENCES users(id),
  content TEXT,
  sent_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT now()
)
```

**Key design decisions explained:**
- The `EXCLUDE USING gist` constraint on `bookings` is a PostgreSQL-native way to **guarantee at the database level** that no two active bookings for the same listing can have overlapping date ranges — this is safer than relying purely on application-level checks, which can race under concurrent requests.
- `amenities` as `JSONB` gives schema flexibility for heterogeneous heritage-home features without needing a rigid amenities table for MVP — can be normalized into a proper `amenities` + `listing_amenities` table later if filtering performance demands it.
- `listing_availability` with a `custom_price` column is what powers festival surge pricing — the booking engine checks this table first before falling back to `listings.price_per_night`.

# 22. ER Diagram Explanation

```
 users ──1:N── listings ──1:N── listing_photos
   │               │
   │               └──1:N── listing_availability
   │
   ├──1:N── bookings ──N:1── listings
   │            │
   │            ├──1:1── payments
   │            ├──1:N── reviews (as booking context)
   │            └──1:1── message_threads ──1:N── messages
   │
   └──N:N── roles (via user_roles)
```

**Explanation:** A `user` can be a host (owns many `listings`) and/or a guest (makes many `bookings`) — the same `users` table serves both roles, distinguished via `user_roles`, rather than separate `hosts` and `guests` tables. This avoids data duplication when a user is both a host and a guest (common in a community-first platform where hosts also travel to other festivals). Each `booking` links exactly one `listing` and one `guest`, and cascades into exactly one `payment` record and one `message_thread`. `reviews` reference `bookings` (not directly listings/users) because a review is *for a specific stay*, and both `reviewer_id`/`reviewee_id` are captured to support the mutual guest↔host review model.

# 27. Security Best Practices

- **Password/OTP security:** OTP rate-limiting (max 5 attempts per 10 min per number) to prevent brute-force; bcrypt/argon2 for any stored password hashes.
- **JWT handling:** Short-lived access tokens, HttpOnly + Secure + SameSite=Strict cookies for refresh tokens; never store tokens in localStorage.
- **Input validation:** Server-side validation via DRF Serializers on every endpoint — never trust client-side validation alone.
- **SQL injection prevention:** Django ORM parameterizes all queries by default — avoid raw SQL (`.raw()`/`.extra()`) unless strictly necessary, and use parameterized queries if you must.
- **File upload security:** Virus-scan uploaded images (e.g., via S3 + Lambda ClamAV), restrict file types/sizes, serve via CDN with signed URLs for private documents (ID verification docs).
- **Payment security:** Never store raw card details — delegate entirely to Razorpay/Stripe's hosted checkout or tokenization; verify all payment webhooks via signature validation.
- **Rate limiting & abuse prevention:** API gateway-level rate limiting (per IP + per user) especially important during festival-season traffic spikes to prevent scraping/bot abuse of listing data.
- **Data privacy:** Encrypt PII fields at rest (phone, ID documents); implement data retention/deletion policies compliant with India's DPDP Act.
- **Dependency security:** Automated dependency scanning (Dependabot/Snyk) in CI pipeline.
- **Admin action audit logging:** Every admin verification/ban/dispute action logged immutably for accountability.

# 28. Scalability Planning

AHHE's defining scalability challenge is **extreme seasonality**, not steady linear growth — traffic can spike 20-50x in the two weeks before a festival and then drop to a fraction of that for months. This shapes scalability strategy differently than a typical SaaS product:

- **Horizontal auto-scaling** of stateless API instances (container orchestration via ECS/Fargate or Kubernetes) tied to CPU/request-rate metrics, scaling aggressively up before known festival dates (predictable spike — can even pre-scale proactively rather than purely reactively).
- **Database read replicas** provisioned ahead of festival windows, decommissioned after, to control cost.
- **Caching layer (Redis)** for listing search results and availability data — search/browse is read-heavy during spikes; cache with short TTL (1-5 min) to massively reduce DB load without serving stale availability for too long.
- **CDN caching** of public listing pages and Pol story pages (mostly static content) to offload origin servers entirely for anonymous browsing traffic.
- **Queue-based async processing** (e.g., BullMQ/Redis queues) for non-critical-path work: notification sending, image processing/resizing, analytics event logging — keeps the booking request/response path fast even under load.
- **Database connection pooling** (PgBouncer) to handle spike-time connection counts without exhausting PostgreSQL's native connection limits.

# 29. Performance Optimization

- **Image optimization:** Resize/convert images server-side on upload (Django + Pillow, or an S3/Lambda pipeline) to WebP with multiple sizes; serve via CDN. Heritage photos are large and high-resolution — this is a real cost/performance factor.
- **Lazy loading:** Below-the-fold listing images and review sections lazy-loaded.
- **ISR (Incremental Static Regeneration):** Listing detail pages regenerated every few hours rather than on every request — balances freshness with performance.
- **Database indexing:** Composite indexes on `(pol_name, status)`, `(host_id)`, and a GiST index supporting the date-range exclusion constraint on bookings; index `listing_availability(listing_id, date)`.
- **N+1 query prevention:** Use Django ORM `select_related()`/`prefetch_related()` for listing+photos+host queries rather than looping queries per listing — a very common Django performance mistake if left unchecked.
- **API response compression:** gzip/brotli at the load balancer level.
- **Debounced search-as-you-type:** Frontend debounces search filter changes (300ms) to avoid hammering the search API on every keystroke.

# 30. Deployment Strategy

**Environments:** `local` → `staging` → `production`, each with isolated databases and environment variables.

**Frontend deployment:** Vercel (or AWS Amplify) — automatic preview deployments per pull request, production deploy on merge to `main`, instant rollback capability.

**Backend deployment:** Containerized (Docker) deployment to ECS Fargate (or Railway/Render for earlier stage) — blue-green deployment strategy so a bad deploy can be instantly rolled back without downtime, which matters most during festival-season windows when downtime = lost bookings during the only high-revenue weeks of the year.

**Database migrations:** Run `python manage.py migrate` as a distinct CI/CD step *before* new application code deploys, with backward-compatible migration discipline (additive changes deployed first, destructive changes only after old code is fully retired) to support zero-downtime deploys.

**Feature flags:** Use a lightweight feature-flag system (e.g., a `feature_flags` table or a service like Unleash) to gate risky new features (e.g., dynamic pricing engine) so they can be toggled off instantly without a redeploy if something misbehaves during peak festival traffic.

# 32. DevOps Workflow

- **Infrastructure as Code:** Terraform manages all cloud resources (VPC, ECS clusters, RDS, S3 buckets, IAM roles) — no manual console changes, ensuring reproducibility and disaster recovery capability.
- **Secrets management:** AWS Secrets Manager or Doppler — never commit secrets to the repo, inject via CI/CD pipeline and container environment variables.
- **Environment parity:** Docker Compose for local dev mirrors staging/production service topology (Postgres, Redis, API, web) so "works on my machine" issues are minimized.
- **On-call rotation:** Even for a small team, a lightweight on-call schedule (PagerDuty free tier or simple phone-alert via Sentry) is essential during the 3 festival windows, when downtime directly costs revenue.
- **Runbooks:** Documented step-by-step recovery procedures for common incidents (payment gateway webhook failures, database connection exhaustion, booking double-conflict edge cases) — written before the first festival season, not during an incident.

# 33. Testing Strategy

| Test Type | Scope | Tooling |
|---|---|---|
| Unit Tests | Business logic (pricing calc, cancellation policy, state machine transitions) | Jest |
| Integration Tests | API endpoints against a real test DB (Dockerized Postgres) | Supertest + Jest |
| E2E Tests | Critical user flows: search → book → pay → confirm | Playwright/Cypress |
| Load Testing | Simulate festival-season traffic spikes on search & booking endpoints | k6 or Artillery |
| Security Testing | OWASP Top 10 checks, dependency vulnerability scans | OWASP ZAP, Snyk |

**Priority order for test coverage:** booking-conflict prevention logic, payment webhook handling, and cancellation/refund calculations should have the highest test coverage (>90%) since bugs here directly cause financial/trust damage — a double-booked heritage home during Uttarayan is a reputation-destroying failure for a trust-dependent platform.

**Pre-festival load testing is mandatory:** Run k6 load tests simulating expected festival-season peak traffic (e.g., 30x normal RPS) at least 3 weeks before each festival, with enough buffer to fix any bottlenecks found.

# 34. Logging & Monitoring

- **Error tracking:** Sentry across frontend and backend, with release tagging so errors can be traced to specific deploys.
- **Application metrics:** Prometheus + Grafana dashboards for API latency, error rate, booking conversion funnel (search → view → request → confirm), payment success rate.
- **Structured logging:** JSON-formatted logs (not plain text) shipped to a centralized log store (CloudWatch Logs or Loki), with correlation IDs threading a single request across services for easier debugging.
- **Business metrics dashboard (non-technical):** A simple admin-facing dashboard tracking GMV, active listings, occupancy rate per festival, host payout totals — for founder/ops visibility, separate from engineering monitoring.
- **Alerting thresholds:** API error rate >2%, p95 latency >1s, payment webhook failure, or database connection pool exhaustion should all trigger immediate alerts, especially during festival windows.

1. **Commission-based revenue (primary):** 5-8% platform fee on each booking — deliberately lower than Airbnb (15-25%) to incentivize adoption and align with the "low-fee, community-first" USP. Even at low margin per booking, festival-season concentration means revenue peaks are still substantial relative to a small operating cost base.
2. **Host subscription tier (optional, Phase 2):** A small monthly/annual fee for hosts wanting premium placement, professional photography service, or multi-listing management tools.
3. **Experience marketplace commission:** A separate, potentially higher commission (10-15%) on bundled experience add-ons (heritage walks, cooking classes) — a genuinely new revenue line hotels/OTAs don't capture as cleanly.
4. **Featured listing placement:** Hosts can pay for boosted visibility during high-competition festival search results (careful design needed so this doesn't undermine trust — clearly labeled as "Featured," not disguised as organic ranking).
5. **Heritage Preservation Fund micro-donations:** Optional guest add-on (e.g., ₹50-100) at checkout, framed as directly funding Pol heritage restoration — this isn't primarily a revenue stream but a **brand differentiation and press/PR magnet** that can indirectly drive acquisition.
6. **Data & insights partnerships (long-term, careful/ethical):** Aggregated, anonymized tourism-pattern insights potentially valuable to Gujarat Tourism Board or heritage NGOs for planning purposes — approached cautiously and transparently, with guest consent.

# 40. Challenges & Risks

- **Trust bootstrapping:** First hosts and guests must trust an unproven platform — mitigated via manual admin verification at launch, founder-led onboarding of the first 20-30 hosts, and strong review-visibility design.
- **Seasonality-driven engineering load:** Building for extreme traffic spikes a few weeks a year while keeping costs low the rest of the year requires deliberate auto-scaling design (see Scalability Planning) rather than over-provisioning year-round.
- **Digital literacy gap:** Older heritage homeowners may need hands-on onboarding support (phone calls, in-person visits) rather than pure self-serve signup — this is an operations/GTM cost, not just a product one.
- **Legal/regulatory ambiguity:** Short-term rental regulations, heritage-property hosting rules, and tax/GST treatment for informal hosts need early legal consultation — non-compliance risk could shut down operations in a worst case.
- **Address/navigation ambiguity:** Pols have narrow, GPS-unfriendly lanes — guest experience can suffer without custom wayfinding solutions (partner with local auto-rickshaw pickup points, custom map annotations).
- **Cash-flow concentration risk:** Most annual revenue may arrive in 3 short windows — requires careful financial planning to sustain operations (salaries, hosting costs) through off-season months.
- **Quality control at scale:** As more hosts join, maintaining the "authentic heritage" bar (not just any spare room, but genuinely characterful heritage stays) requires ongoing curation discipline, not just automated checks.

# 48. Best Practices Followed by Startups

- **Build for the narrow wedge first, expand later:** Don't try to serve all of Ahmedabad tourism on day one — win the Pol heritage homestay niche completely before expanding scope (classic "start narrow, dominate, then expand" playbook used by Airbnb in early SF, Stripe in early developer payments).
- **High-touch onboarding before self-serve at scale:** Manually onboard and verify the first 20-50 hosts personally — automation comes after the trust model and product-market fit are proven, not before.
- **Instrument everything early:** Even basic analytics (funnel drop-off, booking conversion) from day one prevents flying blind on what's actually working.
- **Ship an intentionally incomplete MVP:** Resist scope creep (no native app, no AI pricing, no multi-language beyond 2 languages at first) — validate the core loop (list → search → book → pay → review) before investing in advanced features.
- **Design for trust before scale:** In a marketplace connecting strangers with informal housing, trust infrastructure (verification, reviews, dispute resolution) is not a "nice to have" — it's core product, prioritized alongside the transactional features.
- **Align monetization with community values:** The low-commission model isn't just a pricing choice — it's a trust-building and differentiation strategy against high-fee incumbents.
- **Timed launches around real-world events:** Launching to coincide with a known, high-demand cultural calendar event (festival) creates natural marketing hooks and urgency, rather than a generic "soft launch" with no external tailwind.

# 49. How to Make This Production-Ready

- Complete legal review of short-term rental regulations, heritage-property hosting rules, and applicable GST/tax treatment for host payouts
- Implement automated KYC/eKYC to replace fully manual verification once volume outgrows a small ops team's capacity
- Achieve >80% automated test coverage on booking/payment-critical paths, with load testing validated against realistic festival-season traffic projections

- Conduct a security audit/penetration test before scaling guest volume significantly, given the sensitivity of ID verification data and payment flows
- Validate payout reliability and speed to hosts — trust in a new platform is won or lost on whether hosts actually get paid correctly and on time

# 51. Analytics & Machine Learning Architecture (Dedicated Deep Dive)

Yes — analytics and ML are very much part of AHHE, and given the Django backend, this section lays out concretely how they plug into the stack end-to-end, split into three layers: **product analytics**, **business intelligence**, and **applied machine learning**.

## 51.1 Product & Behavioral Analytics

**Purpose:** Understand what users actually do — search → view → request → book → review funnel drop-off, which Pols/festivals convert best, host onboarding abandonment points.

**Tooling:**
- **PostHog** (self-hostable, generous free tier, session replay + funnels + feature flags in one tool — good fit for a lean team) or **Mixpanel/Google Analytics 4** as alternatives.
- Event tracking implemented via a small `analytics_ml` Django app that exposes a `/api/v1/events/track` endpoint, plus direct frontend event calls (PostHog JS SDK) for pure UI interactions that don't need backend confirmation.
- Key tracked events: `listing_viewed`, `search_performed`, `booking_requested`, `booking_confirmed`, `booking_cancelled`, `host_onboarding_step_completed`, `review_submitted`.

**Business Intelligence layer:**
- Nightly scheduled Django management command (or Celery beat task) aggregates raw events + transactional data into summary tables (`daily_bookings_summary`, `occupancy_by_pol`, `host_funnel_summary`) for fast dashboard queries without hammering the production OLTP tables.
- Dashboards built with **Metabase** (open-source, connects directly to PostgreSQL, minimal setup) for founder/ops-facing business metrics: GMV, active listings, festival occupancy rate, host payout totals (referenced back in Section 34).

## 51.2 Applied Machine Learning — Where It Plugs Into AHHE

| ML Use Case | What It Does | Model Type | When to Build |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dynamic/Seasonal Pricing Suggestions** | Suggests a nightly price range to hosts based on days-to-festival, historical occupancy, comparable listings in the same Pol | Gradient boosting regression (XGBoost/LightGBM) on historical booking + pricing data | Phase 2, once you have 1-2 festival seasons of real booking data |
| **Demand Forecasting** | Predicts booking volume per Pol/area ahead of each festival to guide host-recruitment targeting | Time-series forecasting (Prophet or simple seasonal ARIMA) | Phase 2 |
| **Smart/Conversational Search** | Parses natural-language queries ("cheap rooftop room near Garba ground") into structured filters | LLM-based query parsing (via an API like Claude/GPT, function-calling into your filter schema) — not a model you train yourself | Can be added early; low engineering lift, high UX payoff |
| **Fraud/Anomaly Detection** | Flags suspicious booking patterns (rapid repeat bookings, mismatched payment geography, fake review patterns) | Rule-based system first (simple thresholds), then anomaly detection (Isolation Forest) once enough labeled/observed fraud data exists | Rules at MVP → ML in Phase 2 |
| **Review Summarization** | Summarizes dozens of reviews into key themes on listing pages | LLM summarization (API call), not custom-trained | Easy Phase 2 add |
| **Image Quality/Authenticity Scoring** | Flags likely stock photos or low-quality images that undermine the "authentic heritage" positioning | Pretrained vision model (e.g., CLIP-based similarity scoring, or a simple image-quality heuristic model) | Phase 2/3 |
| **Host Reply Assist** | Suggests draft replies to common guest questions, especially for hosts less comfortable typing in English | LLM-based, prompt-engineered with AHHE-specific context | Phase 2 |
| **Recommendation Engine** | "Guests who booked this Pol also liked…" — surfaces related listings/experiences | Collaborative filtering (matrix factorization) or simple content-based similarity at small data volumes | Phase 2/3, needs meaningful booking volume first |

## 51.3 How ML Actually Gets Served From Django

Two integration patterns, both realistic for a Django shop:

**Pattern A — In-process (simplest, good for MVP-to-Phase-2 scale):**
```
Django app (analytics_ml)
   → loads a trained scikit-learn/XGBoost model (.joblib file) at startup
   → exposes a DRF endpoint, e.g. POST /api/v1/ml/suggest-price
   → model.predict(features) called synchronously inside the view
   → response returned directly to the React frontend
```
This is the right starting point — no extra infrastructure, the model just lives inside the Django deployment. Fine for models that are cheap to run (sub-100ms inference) like price suggestion or fraud scoring.

**Pattern B — Separate ML service (once scale/complexity justifies it):**
```
Django (core app) ──HTTP──▶ FastAPI ML microservice (model serving, e.g. via BentoML/Ray Serve)
```
Split out only if: models become expensive to run (e.g., image models), need GPU inference, need independent scaling from the main API, or the ML team starts iterating faster than the core backend team wants to redeploy Django for. **Don't build Pattern B at MVP — it's premature infrastructure for AHHE's actual scale.**

**Model training/retraining workflow:**
```
Raw data (Postgres: bookings, listings, payments)
   → ml/pipelines/ (pandas feature engineering scripts)
   → ml/notebooks/ (exploration, Jupyter)
   → train model (scikit-learn/XGBoost)
   → evaluate (ml/evaluation/ — backtesting against holdout festival season)
   → serialize model artifact (.joblib)
   → deploy artifact into backend/apps/analytics_ml/models/ (versioned filename, e.g. price_model_v3.joblib)
   → Django loads new artifact on next deploy
```
Retraining cadence: after each festival season (new real-world booking data becomes available in concentrated bursts, which conveniently matches AHHE's natural retraining rhythm).

## 51.4 Practical Sequencing Advice

Don't build ML before you have data. The realistic order for AHHE is:
1. **MVP:** Product analytics (PostHog) + Metabase dashboards only. No ML.
2. **After 1st festival season:** You now have real booking/pricing/occupancy data — first ML candidate is **dynamic pricing suggestions** (highest direct revenue impact, clearest data availability) and **LLM-based smart search/review summarization** (no training data needed, just API calls).
3. **After 2nd festival season:** Demand forecasting, fraud anomaly detection, recommendations — these need more historical volume to be reliable.
4. **Mature stage:** Image authenticity scoring, more sophisticated recommendation systems.

This sequencing avoids the common startup mistake of building ML infrastructure before there's enough data to make it worthwhile — analytics and a Django-servable pricing model are enough "ML story" for a fundraising pitch or a real Phase-2 roadmap without overinvesting early.

# 50. Final Summary

Amdavad Heritage Homestay Exchange (AHHE) is a **deliberately narrow, deeply-differentiated micro-hospitality marketplace** built around three unshakeable pillars: hyperlocal heritage authenticity, festival-native product design, and community-first low-fee economics. Its technical architecture should mirror its business reality — a modular monolith (not premature microservices) that can absorb extreme seasonal traffic spikes cost-efficiently, backed by a relational database whose transactional guarantees make double-booking structurally impossible, and a frontend optimized first for organic SEO discovery, since paid marketing budgets will be limited in the early years.

The single biggest risk is **not technical** — it's the **trust bootstrapping problem** inherent to any marketplace connecting strangers with informal, non-standardized housing stock. Every architectural and product decision in this document — manual verification before automation, mutual-blind reviews, escrow-style payment holding, heritage-authenticity badges, high-touch host onboarding — is designed in service of solving that trust problem first, with scale and automation deliberately deferred until product-market fit is proven.

Done well, AHHE is not just a booking platform — it becomes **economic infrastructure for heritage preservation**, giving Pol families a genuine, recurring financial reason to maintain rather than sell centuries-old homes, while giving travelers access to an authentic cultural experience no hotel chain can replicate. That dual value proposition — commercial viability plus genuine cultural/heritage impact — is what makes AHHE a defensible, mission-aligned business rather than just another regional Airbnb clone.
